# Common Chess Openings — Quick Reference

Short, principle-focused notes on well-known openings, meant to ground move
selection with general strategic character rather than deep theory.

## Italian Game (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4)
Develops the bishop to an active diagonal aimed at f7, White's most
vulnerable point early on. Favors quick development and early piece activity
over immediate central pawn tension.

## Ruy Lopez / Spanish Opening (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5)
Pressures the knight that defends the e5 pawn. One of the oldest and most
respected openings, typically leading to rich, long-term strategic
middlegames rather than early tactics.

## Sicilian Defense (1.e4 c5)
Black's most popular and combative reply to 1.e4. Creates an asymmetrical
pawn structure and fights for the center from the flank rather than meeting
e4 head-on, generally leading to sharper, unbalanced positions.

## French Defense (1.e4 e6)
Solid and resilient; Black challenges the center immediately with ...d5, but
the light-squared bishop can become passive, hemmed in by Black's own pawn
chain.

## Caro-Kann Defense (1.e4 c6)
Also aims for ...d5, but keeps the light-squared bishop free (unlike the
French). Known for durability and a low-risk pawn structure.

## Queen's Gambit (1.d4 d5 2.c4)
White offers a flank pawn to gain central control and development tempo.
"Accepted" (Black takes the pawn) and "Declined" (Black holds the center)
lead to very different middlegame characters.

## King's Indian Defense (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6)
A hypermodern setup: Black lets White build a big pawn center, then strikes
back later with ...e5 or ...c5, often aiming for a kingside attack.

## English Opening (1.c4)
A flexible flank opening that frequently transposes into other structures.
Popular for sidestepping heavily analyzed main-line theory.

## Scandinavian Defense (1.e4 d5)
Challenges the center immediately. After 2.exd5 Qxd5, Black's queen comes
out early and may lose time to further development, but the position stays
solid and easy to understand.

## Petrov's (Russian) Defense (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6)
A symmetrical, solid reply valued for its safety — historically known for a
drawish reputation at the highest level.

## General opening principles
Control the center (with pawns or pieces), develop minor pieces before
moving the same piece twice, castle early for king safety, and avoid
bringing the queen out too early where it can be attacked with tempo.
